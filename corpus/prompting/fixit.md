# FIXIT

A five-part framework for everyday writing tasks. Give AI exactly enough to deliver what you want — first time.

---

## What FIXIT stands for

| Letter | Element | The question it answers | When it matters most |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F** | Format | What should the output look like? | Always — default output is rarely the right format |
| **I** | Instructions | What should AI actually do? | Always — topic is not the same as action |
| **X** | eXamples | What does good look like? | When tone and style matter — worth more than any description |
| **I** | Intent | What is this for, and who is it for? | When the audience changes the tone |
| **T** | Tone | What is the right register? | When the text is external or high-stakes |

---

## See the difference

**Without FIXIT:**

> Write something about our new product.

AI will produce something. It will be generic and probably not what you had in mind.

**With FIXIT:**

> Write a product description of maximum 80 words [Format] for our webshop [Intent]. Use this structure: benefit, application, call to action [Instructions]. Here is an example of a text we like: [paste example] [eXamples]. The tone should be direct and down-to-earth — no marketing language [Tone].

Same task. Completely different result. The second prompt takes thirty seconds longer to write and saves you ten minutes of back-and-forth.

---

## When to use FIXIT

FIXIT works best for writing tasks where the format and tone matter. It is broad enough to cover most everyday content needs.

Use it for: product descriptions, social media posts, email drafts, blog outlines, internal announcements, summaries, rewritten texts.

If your task is primarily about reaching a specific audience or producing external communication, CO-STAR may be a better fit — it puts more emphasis on who the reader is and what effect the text should have. If your task is a plan, an analysis, or a structured process, consider RISEN instead.

---

## A few things worth knowing

**You do not have to use every element.** FIXIT is a checklist, not a formula. For a simple task, Format and Instructions may be enough. For a complex one, all five elements earn their place.

**Order does not matter.** You do not need to write F then I then X then I then T in sequence. Write the prompt naturally. Use FIXIT as a mental check before you hit enter: have I covered the important parts?

**The X is optional but powerful.** If you have a text that is close to what you want, paste it in. Even a rough example will steer the output more precisely than a written description of the style.
