# CO-STAR

The framework that starts with the reader, not the task.

Write for someone, not everyone.

When the same product needs to speak differently to a farmer, an agronomist, and a buyer — CO-STAR is the framework that handles that.

---

## What CO-STAR stands for

| Letter | Element | The question it answers | When it matters most |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C** | Context | What is the background? | When AI needs to understand the situation before it can write |
| **O** | Objective | What should the text achieve? | Always — goal drives everything else |
| **S** | Style | What writing style fits? | When the format has a specific convention (press release, landing page, newsletter) |
| **T** | Tone | What is the right register? | When the emotional register matters — trusted advisor vs urgent call to action |
| **A** | Audience | Who is reading this? | Always for external content — the reader shapes the language |
| **R** | Response | What format should the answer be in? | Always — length, structure, number of paragraphs |

---

## See the difference

**Without CO-STAR:**

> Write an intro text for our landing page about the new fertiliser product.

Generic, flat, and probably too long. AI does not know who it is writing for or what the text should make the reader do.

**With CO-STAR:**

> We are launching a new fertiliser solution for grain producers [Context]. Write an intro text for the landing page that gets visitors to book a demo [Objective]. Write in a clear, professional style without jargon [Style]. The tone should be confident and advisory [Tone]. The audience is Danish farmers with medium-sized operations [Audience]. Respond in two paragraphs of maximum 60 words each [Response].

Same task. The second prompt gives AI everything it needs to write for a specific reader with a specific goal.

---

## When to use CO-STAR

CO-STAR works best when the audience is the key variable — when the same message needs to land differently depending on who is reading it.

Use it for: landing pages, campaign texts, newsletters, press releases, product descriptions for external audiences, customer emails, website copy.

If your task is primarily about format and tone rather than audience, FIXIT may be simpler and faster. If your task is a structured deliverable like a plan or an analysis, use RISEN instead.

---

## CO-STAR vs FIXIT

The two models overlap more than they differ. Both ask about tone and format. The real distinction is emphasis.

FIXIT starts with the output: what format, what instructions, what examples. CO-STAR starts with the reader: who is this for, what should it do to them, what context do they need.

If you find yourself thinking about the audience first, use CO-STAR. If you find yourself thinking about the format first, use FIXIT. If you are not sure, either will get you most of the way there.

---

## A few things worth knowing

**Audience is the most important element.** The A in CO-STAR does more work than any other letter. A text for a farmer with a medium-sized operation reads differently than one for an agronomist at a large cooperative — even if the product is the same. Be specific.

**Objective and Response are easy to overlook.** Most people remember to set the tone. Fewer remember to say what the text should *do* (book a demo, understand a benefit, take action) and how long it should be. Both matter.

**You do not have to choose between CO-STAR and FIXIT.** Use the one that fits the task. Some people build hybrid prompts that pull elements from both. That is completely fine — the models are tools, not rules.
