# RISEN

Most prompts ask for a result. RISEN asks for a process.

Structure is not a constraint. It is a shortcut.

When you define the path, AI stops guessing and starts delivering.

---

## What RISEN stands for

| Letter | Element | The question it answers | When it matters most |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R** | Role | What role should AI play? | When expertise or perspective shapes the output |
| **I** | Instructions | What should AI do? | Always — the core task |
| **S** | Steps | What is the path to the result? | When structure and sequence matter |
| **E** | End goal | What does the finished result look like? | When the deliverable has a specific purpose or audience |
| **N** | Narrowing | What should be avoided or limited? | Always — constraints save you iterations |

---

## See the difference

**Without RISEN:**

> Help me make a project plan for a website migration.

AI will produce something. It may be structured, it may not. The length, depth, and format are anyone's guess.

**With RISEN:**

> You are an experienced project manager [Role]. Help me structure a project plan for a website migration [Instructions]. Start with an overall timeline, then the key milestones, and finish with a risk assessment [Steps]. The end result should be a plan I can present to management next week [End goal]. Keep it to maximum two pages and avoid technical jargon [Narrowing].

Same task. The second prompt defines the path, not just the destination. AI follows your structure instead of inventing its own.

---

## When to use RISEN

RISEN works best when the output is more than a text — when it is a structured work result that needs to follow a specific logic or sequence.

Use it for: project plans, analyses, step-by-step guides, reports, risk assessments, onboarding documents, process descriptions, meeting agendas.

If your task is primarily a piece of writing with a specific format and tone, FIXIT is faster. If the audience is the main variable, use CO-STAR. RISEN is for the tasks where the structure of the answer is what matters.

---

## The two elements that make RISEN different

**Steps** is what separates RISEN from the other models. Instead of just asking for a result, you define the path to it. This gives you much more control over the structure of the answer — and it means AI is less likely to produce something that looks complete but follows the wrong logic.

Think of it as the difference between asking a colleague to write a report and asking them to write a report that starts with a summary, covers three specific themes, and ends with a recommendation. The second brief produces a more useful result because the structure is defined upfront.

**Narrowing** is the element people most often leave out — and skipping it is expensive. Narrowing is where you tell AI what you do not want. No bullet points. Maximum two pages. Avoid technical jargon. No recommendations, just facts.

Every constraint you add in Narrowing is one fewer iteration you need to do afterwards. Think of it as the guardrails that keep the output on track.

---

## A few things worth knowing

**Role is optional but powerful for specialist tasks.** Telling AI it is an experienced project manager, a senior communications advisor, or a legal reviewer changes the register and depth of the output. Use it when expertise matters. Skip it for everyday tasks.

**Steps does not mean a rigid sequence.** You are not writing a recipe. Steps is a way of telling AI what the major sections or phases should be. Two or three anchors are enough — AI will fill in the detail.

**Narrowing is worth spending time on.** Before you send the prompt, ask yourself: what would make this answer useless? Then rule it out in the Narrowing element. One sentence of constraints can save ten minutes of rewriting.
